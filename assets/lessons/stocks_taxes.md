# Tax on equity investments

> Tax rules change in almost every Budget. The figures below were the rules after the July 2024 Budget. Check the Income Tax department's site or a CA before you act.

## Capital gains on shares and equity funds

| Held for | Type | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months or less | **STCG** (short-term capital gain) | 20% |
| More than 12 months | **LTCG** (long-term capital gain) | 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year |

STT (securities transaction tax) is charged on every trade on top of these.

## Dividends

Dividends are added to your income and taxed at your **slab rate**.

## Other investments

- **Debt mutual funds** (bought from April 2023): gains are taxed at your slab rate however long you hold them.
- **Gold funds and ETFs**: rules differ by product and holding period. Sovereign Gold Bonds held to maturity were tax-free, but new issues have been paused.

## ELSS and Section 80C

ELSS funds give a deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh under 80C, with a 3-year lock-in. **This only helps in the old tax regime.** The new regime, which is now the default, has no 80C deduction. The **Plan** tab's tax switch swaps your flexi cap slice for ELSS.

## Tax-gain harvesting

Each year you can realise up to ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains tax-free. Sell units with gains, then buy them back. This raises your purchase price, which reduces tax later. Do it before 31 March.

## F&O income

Profit or loss from futures and options is **business income**, taxed at your slab rate. You may need a tax audit and an ITR-3 filing. This is one more cost of trading.

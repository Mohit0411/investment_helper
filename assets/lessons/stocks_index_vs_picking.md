# Index funds vs picking stocks

## What an index fund is

The Nifty 50 is a list of India's 50 largest listed companies. A **Nifty 50 index fund** simply owns all 50 in the same proportions. No fund manager picks stocks, so costs are tiny (often around 0.1–0.2% a year on direct plans).

## Why index funds are hard to beat

- **Costs add up**: a 1.5% annual fee compounds into a big gap over 20 years.
- **Most active funds trail their benchmark** over long periods, especially in large caps. S&P's SPIVA India reports track this every year.
- **No single company can sink you**: if one company collapses, it is only a small part of the index.
- **No skill or time needed**: you get the market's return.

## When picking stocks makes sense

- You enjoy the research and will actually read annual reports.
- You keep it to a **satellite**: about 10–20% of your portfolio, with the core in funds.
- You hold 10–15 stocks across sectors, not 2 or 3.
- You can sit through a 40% fall without panic-selling.

## Core and satellite

| Part | Share | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| Core | 80–90% | Index funds, flexi cap funds, debt, gold (that's what the **Plan** tab builds) |
| Satellite | 10–20% | Your own stock picks, a sector idea, learning money |

## Mistakes to avoid

- Buying because of a tip from a Telegram group, YouTube video or a friend.
- **Averaging down** on a falling stock without checking whether the business has changed.
- Checking prices every hour. More activity usually means worse returns.

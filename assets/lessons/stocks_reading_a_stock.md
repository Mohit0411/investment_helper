# Reading a stock page

Open any stock on a site like Screener.in or Tickertape and you'll see a wall of numbers. These are the ones that matter most.

## Valuation: is it cheap or expensive?

- **P/E (price to earnings)** = share price ÷ earnings per share. A P/E of 25 means you pay ₹25 for every ₹1 of yearly profit. Compare it with the company's own history and with peers in the same sector, never across sectors.
- **P/B (price to book)**: price compared with net assets. Most useful for banks and financial companies.
- **Market cap** = share price × number of shares. Large cap is roughly the top 100 companies by market cap, mid cap is the next 150, and small cap is everything below.

> A high P/E is not automatically bad. It means the market expects fast growth. If that growth doesn't come, the price usually falls hard.

## Quality: is it a good business?

- **EPS growth**: is profit per share rising year after year?
- **ROE (return on equity)**: profit ÷ shareholders' money. Consistently **above 15%** is a good sign.
- **ROCE**: like ROE, but it includes debt. Useful for capital-heavy businesses.
- **Debt to equity**: below 0.5 is comfortable for most non-financial companies. High debt makes bad years much worse.
- **Operating cash flow**: profits should turn into actual cash. If profit keeps rising but cash doesn't, be careful.

## Ownership

- **Promoter holding**: founders owning a big stake is usually good. Watch out for a high share of **pledged** shares.
- **FII/DII holding**: foreign and domestic institutions buying in is a vote of confidence.

## A simple checklist

1. Can you explain what the company sells, and why customers choose it?
2. Has profit grown for at least 5 years?
3. ROE above 15%, debt to equity below 0.5?
4. Is the P/E reasonable compared with its own history and its peers?

If you can't answer these, an index fund is the better choice. That's the next lesson.

# Using options to protect investments

For a long-term investor, the most useful thing about options is **insurance**, not speculation.

## Protective put

You hold about ₹18 lakh of Nifty-linked funds and you're worried about a fall over the next 2 months (an election, say).

1. One Nifty lot at 24,000 × 75 is ₹18 lakh, so one put roughly matches your holding. (Smaller portfolios can't be hedged exactly, because you can't buy part of a lot.)
2. Buy one Nifty **put** about 4% below the current level (23,000) for 2 months.
3. If Nifty falls 15% to 20,400, your funds lose about ₹2.7 lakh. The put is worth (23,000 − 20,400) × 75 = ₹1.95 lakh. Your net loss is about ₹75,000 plus the premium, however far the market falls.
4. If Nifty rises, you lose only the premium. That's the insurance cost.

Like any insurance, you **hope not to use it**, and it isn't free. Hedging every month costs a lot over the years. Most long-term investors are better off simply holding the right **asset allocation**, which is what the Plan tab does. Use hedges for specific, short windows.

## Covered call

If you hold shares and expect them to move sideways, you can sell an OTM call each month to collect premium. The trade-off is that if the stock rallies past the strike, your upside is capped.

## Simpler hedges that need no F&O

- **Rebalancing**: selling some equity after a big rally automatically locks in gains.
- **Gold and debt** in the portfolio: they often hold up when equities fall.
- **Emergency fund**: so you never have to sell equity in a crash.

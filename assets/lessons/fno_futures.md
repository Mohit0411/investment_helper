# Derivatives and futures

A **derivative** is a contract whose value comes from something else: an index, a stock, gold. In India, "F&O" means **futures and options** traded on NSE/BSE.

## Futures

A future is an agreement to buy or sell something at a fixed price on a fixed date (**expiry**).

- You trade in **lots**: a fixed quantity set by the exchange. NSE revises lot sizes from time to time, so check the current one.
- You don't pay the full value. You put up **margin**, usually around 10–20% of the contract value.
- Gains and losses are settled **every day** (mark-to-market). If the market goes against you, money leaves your account that evening.

## Leverage: the double-edged sword

Say Nifty is at 24,000 and the lot size is 75. One lot controls ₹18 lakh of Nifty. If the margin is about ₹1.5 lakh, that's roughly **12x leverage**.

| Nifty moves | P&L on ₹18L position | As % of your ₹1.5L margin |
|---|---|---|
| +2% | +₹36,000 | **+24%** |
| −2% | −₹36,000 | **−24%** |
| −8% | −₹1,44,000 | **−96%** |

A 2% day happens several times a year. With leverage, normal market noise can wipe out your capital.

## Margin calls

If losses eat into your margin, the broker asks you to add money. If you don't, they **close your position at a loss**, often at the worst moment.

## Why futures exist

Futures were built for **hedging**. A gold importer can lock in today's price, and a fund can protect a portfolio. Speculating with them is possible, but it's the riskiest way to use them.

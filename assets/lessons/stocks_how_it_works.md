# How the stock market works

A **share** is a small piece of ownership in a company. If a company has 100 crore shares and you own 100, you own a tiny slice of its profits, assets and future.

## Who's involved

| Player | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Exchange** (NSE, BSE) | The marketplace where buy and sell orders are matched |
| **Broker** (Zerodha, Groww, Upstox…) | Sends your orders to the exchange |
| **Depository** (CDSL, NSDL) | Holds your shares electronically in your **demat account** |
| **SEBI** | The regulator that sets the rules for everyone |

Your shares sit in your demat account, **not** with the broker. If a broker shuts down, your shares are still yours.

## What moves a price

Every trade has a buyer and a seller. The price moves when one side is more eager than the other. Over the short term, news and sentiment drive prices. Over years, prices follow **earnings**: companies that grow profits tend to see their share price grow too.

## Placing an order

- **Market order**: buy or sell immediately at the best available price. Fast, but the price can slip.
- **Limit order**: buy or sell only at your price or better. You control the price, but it may not fill.
- **Stop-loss**: an order that triggers once the price hits a level you set, to limit a loss.

**Delivery (CNC)** means you keep the shares. **Intraday (MIS)** means you must close the position the same day. Intraday trading is speculation, not investing.

## Settlement

India settles trades on **T+1**: shares bought on Monday reach your demat on Tuesday, and money from a sale reaches you the next working day.

## Key idea

The stock market is a place to **own businesses**. Over 10+ years, owning good businesses has beaten fixed deposits and inflation. Over 10 days it is close to a coin toss.
